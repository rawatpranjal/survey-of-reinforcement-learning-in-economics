% ------------------------------------------------------------- 2. two traditions
\begin{frame}{Two traditions}

Economics and reinforcement learning both study sequential decision-making under
uncertainty. They descend from different intellectual traditions, and they share a
vocabulary whose meanings diverge.

\bigskip
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
 & \color{rlblue}Inference culture & \color{rlorange}Control culture \\
\midrule
Central task
  & understand the world
  & act in the world \\[0.7em]
Effort goes to
  & recovering the primitives; the optimal policy is a byproduct
  & taking objective and dynamics as given; computing and deploying the policy \\[0.7em]
The enterprise
  & demand estimation, dynamic discrete choice
  & PID, LQR, MPC, RL \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------ 3. the word "model"
\begin{frame}{What ``model'' means}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\textcolor{rlblue}{\textbf{Inference tradition}}

\medskip
A specification of preferences, beliefs, constraints, and an equilibrium concept.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\textcolor{rlorange}{\textbf{Reinforcement learning}}

\medskip
A transition kernel $P(s' \mid s,a)$ and a reward function $r(s,a)$. Nothing else.
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\bigskip
\bigskip
\begin{quote}
\itshape
To an econometrician, a model is a set of falsifiable restrictions on the joint
distribution of observables and primitives. To an RL researcher, a model is a
simulator you can call.
\end{quote}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------- 4. prediction and control
\begin{frame}{Prediction and control}

\textbf{\textcolor{rlblue}{Prediction}}\quad In RL this means estimating $V^\pi(s)$
or $Q^\pi(s,a)$ for a fixed policy $\pi$, that is, policy evaluation. It does not
mean forecasting observable variables.

\bigskip
\textbf{\textcolor{rlblue}{Control}}\quad In RL this means finding the policy
$\pi^\star$ that maximises expected discounted return, that is, policy
optimisation. It does not mean the inclusion of regressors.

\bigskip
Because prediction concerns evaluating a specific policy, the next question is
whether the data was generated by the policy being studied.
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------- 5. on-policy and off-policy
\begin{frame}{On-policy and off-policy}

\textbf{\textcolor{rlblue}{On-policy}}\quad Methods that evaluate and improve the
same policy that generates the data.

\bigskip
\textbf{\textcolor{rlblue}{Off-policy}}\quad Methods that learn about a target
policy $\pi$ from data generated by a different behavioural policy $\mu$.

\bigskip
Off-policy evaluation is precisely counterfactual policy evaluation. What would
happen if we deployed $\pi$ instead of $\mu$?
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------ 6. online, offline, real-time
\begin{frame}{Online, offline, real-time}

\textbf{\textcolor{rlblue}{Offline}}\quad Also called batch. Learning exclusively
from a fixed dataset of previously collected transitions, with no ability to gather
more. This setting is closer to standard empirical work than the online one.

\bigskip
\textbf{\textcolor{rlblue}{Online}}\quad Learning while interacting, collecting new
data as a consequence of one's own actions. It carries \alert{no timing
constraint}. It corresponds to adaptive experimental design, not to speed.

\bigskip
\textbf{\textcolor{rlblue}{Real-time}}\quad Hard deadlines on the perception-action
loop, as in robotics. Every real-time RL system is online, but most online RL
(games, recommenders) is not real-time.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------- 7. model-based and model-free
\begin{frame}{Model-based and model-free}

The labels refer only to whether the algorithm constructs or is given an explicit
representation of $P$ and $r$. A model-based algorithm plans through that
representation, as AlphaZero does with the known rules of the game. A model-free
algorithm computes values or policies directly from experienced transitions.

\bigskip
\alert{Neither maps onto reduced-form versus structural.} The labels say nothing
about whether the analyst makes structural assumptions about preferences or
equilibrium.

\bigskip
``Model-free'' does not mean assumption-free either. Both variants operate within
an MDP, which already imposes the Markov assumption on the state.

\bigskip
A rich structural model can be specified and then solved with a model-free
algorithm used purely as a numerical tool.
\end{frame}

% -------------------------------------------------- 8. training and execution
\begin{frame}{Training and execution}

Every RL system passes through two phases. In the \textcolor{rlblue}{training
phase} the agent interacts with an environment and updates its parameters. In the
\textcolor{rlblue}{execution phase} the policy is frozen and used to decide without
further updates.

\bigskip
This is what makes ``online'' hard to read. Online training almost always takes
place inside a simulator, not in the world. AlphaGo Zero trained online through
millions of self-play games; when it faced Lee Sedol its weights were frozen and it
was purely executing.

\bigskip
\textbf{\textcolor{rlorange}{In-field}}\quad Not standard RL vocabulary. Introduced
here for genuine live-market learning, where exploration has real financial cost,
as against the far more common case of online learning inside a simulator.
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------ 9. "inference" overloaded
\begin{frame}{``Inference'' is overloaded}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\textcolor{rlorange}{\textbf{In machine learning}}

\medskip
Executing a frozen model. A forward pass producing outputs from inputs.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\textcolor{rlblue}{\textbf{In statistics}}

\medskip
Standard errors, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests.
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\bigskip
This survey uses ``inference'' exclusively in the statistical sense, and
``execution'' or ``deployment'' for applying a trained model.

\bigskip
The stakes are not merely verbal. Most RL convergence results and sample-complexity
guarantees refer to the \alert{training} phase. Reading them as claims about
deployed performance requires a separate argument.
\end{frame}

% --------------------------------------------------------------- 12. policy
\begin{frame}{Policy}

\textcolor{rlblue}{\textbf{Inference tradition}}\quad A rule set by a government,
central bank, or regulator: a tax schedule, a subsidy, an interest-rate rule, a
licensing requirement. It is part of the \emph{environment} in which private agents
optimise. ``Policy evaluation'' means changing the environment and asking how
agents would respond.

\bigskip
\textcolor{rlorange}{\textbf{Reinforcement learning}}\quad The agent's own decision
rule $\pi(a \mid s)$. ``Policy evaluation'' means computing $V^\pi(s)$, the expected
return from following that rule in a \alert{fixed} environment.

\bigskip
The word is overloaded, and the two readings hold opposite things fixed. RL usually
assumes a high-fidelity simulator whose rules do not change, and treats environment
shift as a nuisance (sim-to-real, domain adaptation) rather than as the object of
study. Offline RL is the exception, where distributional shift between the training
data and the deployed environment becomes a central concern.
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------------ 13. identification
\begin{frame}{Identification}

\textcolor{rlblue}{\textbf{Inference tradition}}\quad A parameter $\theta$ is identified if
it is uniquely pinned down by the data-generating process together with the model's
maintained assumptions, so that no two distinct values generate the same
distribution of observables \citep{Lewbel2019}. This is a \alert{logical} property
of the model, not a statistical one. If it fails, no amount of additional data
recovers $\theta$.

\bigskip
\textcolor{rlorange}{\textbf{Reinforcement learning}}\quad There is little general
identification discourse. The analogs are narrow: reward identifiability in inverse
RL \citep{KimGarg2021}, and system identification inherited from control theory
\citep{RossBagnell2012}.

\bigskip
The reason is a difference in what counts as success. RL grades out-of-sample
performance of the learned controller, so what matters is that the policy
generalises.
\end{frame}

% --------------------------------------------------------------- 14. bandit
\begin{frame}{Bandit}

The word carries different mathematical content in each field.

\bigskip
\textcolor{rlblue}{\textbf{Inference tradition}}\quad A Bayesian sequential allocation
problem \citep{Gittins1979}. The state is the posterior over unknown arm
distributions and the solution is the Gittins index, from the theory of optimal
stopping.

\bigskip
\textcolor{rlorange}{\textbf{Reinforcement learning}}\quad A frequentist
regret-minimisation problem. Algorithms such as UCB provide worst-case bounds on cumulative regret
$\sum_{t=1}^T (\mu^\star - \mu_{A_t})$ without requiring priors.

\bigskip
The two traditions ask different questions, Bayesian optimality of the full
sequential problem against minimax regret rates, and their answers are
\alert{not directly comparable}.
\end{frame}

% ----------------------------------------------------- 15. contextual bandit
\begin{frame}{Contextual bandit}

To a reader from the inference tradition the ``context'' is just the state variable
$x_t$, and a contextual bandit looks like an MDP with an unknown reward parameter.

\bigskip
What the RL literature signals by ``context'' is a structural restriction on
transitions,
\[
  P(x_{t+1} \mid x_t, a_t) = P(x_{t+1}),
\]
so the agent's action has \alert{no causal effect} on the next context.

\bigskip
The restriction separates the exploration problem from the planning problem. With
exogenous contexts there is no long-horizon credit assignment, and the problem
reduces to repeated one-period optimisation under uncertainty.

\bigskip
``Contextual'' therefore flags a modelling assumption about dynamics, not merely
the presence of observable covariates.
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------------------- 16. the agent's world
\begin{frame}{The agent's world}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
 & \color{rlblue}Inference tradition & \color{rlorange}Reinforcement learning \\
\midrule
Agent
  & a human decision-maker (consumer, worker, firm) or a planner, whose choices are
    the object of study
  & the learning algorithm itself; the human, if present, is part of the
    environment providing reward signals \\
Environment
  & market structure, institutional rules, used loosely
  & a formal object: everything outside the agent \\
Reward, utility
  & utility $u(x,d)$ is a primitive of preferences, to be recovered from observed
    choices
  & reward $r(s,a)$ is a known, externally specified function the algorithm
    maximises \\
Outcome
  & the dependent variable $Y$ in a regression
  & no single analog: $r_t$ per period, the return $G_t$ cumulative, $V^\pi(s)$ in
    expectation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------- 17. learning and exploring
\begin{frame}[shrink=15]{Learning and exploring}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
 & \color{rlblue}Inference tradition & \color{rlorange}Reinforcement learning \\
\midrule
Learning
  & agents forming and refining beliefs about unknown parameters
  & primarily \alert{computation}: stochastic approximation to the fixed point of
    the Bellman operator, not human-like learning \\
Active learning
  & Bayesian experimentation; the cost of exploration traded against the option
    value of better future decisions
  & in machine learning, a supervised protocol for choosing which unlabelled
    examples to query an oracle about \\
Exploration
  & optimal experimentation, where the value of information is derived endogenously
    from the dynamic program
  & a modular subcomponent, crude ($\varepsilon$-greedy) or principled (UCB,
    Thompson sampling) \\
Bootstrapping
  & Efron's resampling method \citep{Efron1979}
  & updating a value estimate using another value estimate rather than a complete
    realised return \citep{sutton1988} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{frame}

% ------------------------------------------ 18. words borrowed from statistics
\begin{frame}[shrink=15]{Words borrowed from statistics}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
 & \color{rlblue}Inference tradition & \color{rlorange}Reinforcement learning \\
\midrule
Function approx.
  & sieve estimation, nonparametric series estimation
  & the same thing: value functions or policies on a finite-dimensional basis \\
Calibration
  & choosing parameters to match empirical moments or stylised facts
  & in machine learning, making predicted probabilities match observed
    frequencies \\
Regret
  & an emotion modifying preferences, or a static minimax decision criterion
  & a dynamic metric: cumulative gap to the best fixed policy in hindsight \\
Efficiency
  & welfare. Pareto efficiency, allocative efficiency
  & resources. Sample efficiency, computational efficiency \\
Discount factor
  & a structural primitive encoding time preference, derived axiomatically
    \citep{Koopmans1960}
  & a hyperparameter keeping the Bellman operator a contraction; an optimizing
    representation \citep{Pitis2019} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{frame}

\srcnote{Three collisions to watch. $u$ denotes both the control $u_t$ and
