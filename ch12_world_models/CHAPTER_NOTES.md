### `cobweb_paradigms.py` — Cobweb with adjustment cost, §9.1

- Env: `cobweb_env.py`, self-referential price $p_t = a - b q_t + \varepsilon_t$, reward $r_t = p_t q_t - (c/2) q_t^2 - (\phi/2)(q_t - q_{t-1})^2$, oracle via LQ-Bellman fixed-point iteration. Sweep over $b/c \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ (stable/borderline/unstable), 20 seeds, $T = 500$.
- **Seven paradigms** in rank order by mean regret: Oracle, RLS, Model-Based LQ (closed-form Riccati on point estimates), Arifovic GA (no election operator), Naive (fixed $q = 1.4$), MBPO (bootstrap-ensemble dynamics + branched rollouts + REINFORCE on $K_0, K_q$), Q-Learning. The split between "Model-Based LQ" and "full MBPO" was added late in May 2026 to disambiguate closed-form planning from REINFORCE-through-imagination.
- Key finding (the "inversion" written into the §9 verdict): RLS wins the regret race because its early actions are nearly optimal under correct functional form, but Model-Based LQ wins the asymptotic policy-distance comparison because it actually fits $(c, \phi)$ rather than assuming them.
- Outputs: `cobweb_paradigms.png` (3-panel regret curves), `cobweb_paradigms_param_recovery.png` (3 rows × 2 cols, $\hat a_t$ and $\hat b_t$ trajectories), `cobweb_paradigms_policy_distance.png` (log-scale policy distance to oracle), `cobweb_paradigms_results.tex`, `cobweb_paradigms_final_recovery.tex`, `cobweb_paradigms_stdout.txt`, audit at `cobweb_paradigms_audit.md`.

### `fishery_paradigms.py` — Logistic-growth fishery, §9.2

- Env: `fishery_env.py`, $s_{t+1} = \max(0, s_t + r s_t (1 - s_t / K) - h_t + \varepsilon_t)$, reward $r_t = p h_t - (c/2) h_t^2$. Parameters $r = 0.4$, $K = 10$, $p = 2$, $c = 0.2$, $\sigma = 0.3$, $\gamma = 0.95$, $T = 500$, 20 seeds, $s_0 = K$.
- Oracle: grid-based discounted DP via `solve_oracle_dp` (50 stock × 25 action points, 9-point Gauss-Hermite quadrature for the Gaussian transition noise). Inner DP refits in RLS/MBPO use coarser 25 × 15 grids with 60-iter cap for speed; refit every 25 observations.
- **Six paradigms** in rank order: Oracle, RLS, Model-Based LQ, Q-Learning, Naive, Arifovic GA. Full MBPO with REINFORCE is omitted because the non-linear logistic dynamics make a closed-form policy parameterization unavailable.
- Headline numbers (final regret at $T = 500$, mean ± SE): 0.00, 13.67 ± 0.43, 14.69 ± 0.73, 274.71 ± 24.36, 447.35 ± 3.24, 706.13 ± 16.65.
- Surprise: Q-Learning beats Naive and Arifovic GA on this env (the discretized grid happens to align with the optimal feedback rule's monotone structure, while the GA's constant-harvest functional form is wrong for an environment where the optimal action varies smoothly with stock). The §9.2 verdict names this explicitly.
- Outputs: `fishery_paradigms.png`, `fishery_paradigms_results.tex`, `fishery_paradigms_stdout.txt`, audit at `fishery_paradigms_audit.md`.

## §1 walkthrough — the economics catalogue

The opening paragraph (no TODO any more) establishes that "world models are learned perceived laws of motion" and pivots to the catalogue. §1.1 then walks through five economics approaches in this order:

1. **Belief-based learning from game theory.** Fictitious play (Brown 1951), smooth fictitious play (Fudenberg-Kreps 1993), stochastic fictitious play with global convergence (Hofbauer-Sandholm 2002), textbook references Fudenberg-Levine 1998 and Young 2004. The framing is: economists already know belief-updating dynamics, model-based RL is in the same family.
2. **Recursive least squares adaptive learning.** Marcet-Sargent 1989, Evans-Honkapohja 2001. Linear law of motion with RLS updates; E-stability ODE governs convergence; no exploration mechanism.
3. **Genetic algorithm learning.** Arifovic 1994 (cobweb), 1995 (OLG). Population evolution with election operator that admits offspring only if they beat parents on the previous environment. Converges where RLS diverges.
4. **Evolutionary heuristic switching.** Brock-Hommes 1997. Logit-on-past-performance choice over a finite menu of heuristics. Period-doubling cascade as intensity-of-choice rises.
5. **Misspecified Bayesian learning.** Esponda-Pouzo 2016 Berk-Nash equilibrium. Beliefs concentrate on KL-minimizing parameters within a misspecified model class; misspecification is permanent rather than transient.

Closing paragraph (the merged former §1.2) discusses single-agent vs multi-agent convergence questions and pivots to §2.

The catalogue is deliberately compact — the chapter is not a survey of economics learning models, just a reminder for the economist reader that the model-based-RL line slots into a family they already know.

## §2 walkthrough — the two 1990 origins in proper notation

Two subsubsections, treated in parallel rather than as lineage.

### §2.1 Sutton 1990 (Dyna)

Defers the full algorithm box to §3. Stresses the architectural openness: the model can be "probabilistic and oftentimes incorrect", and the same outer loop accepts tabular counts, Gaussian processes, deep ensembles, RSSM latents, or implicit value-equivalent latents.

### §2.2 Schmidhuber 1990 (controller-model)

Now carries proper formalism:

- World model $M_\theta: (s_t, a_t) \mapsto p_\theta(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t)$, parameterized by $\theta$.
- Controller $C_\phi$: $\pi_\phi(a_t \mid s_t)$, parameterized by $\phi$.
- Model loss $\mathcal{L}_M(\theta) = -\sum_t \log p_\theta(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t)$ on observed transitions.
- Controller objective $J(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{a_t \sim \pi_\phi, s_{t+1} \sim M_\theta}\!\left[\sum_t \gamma^t r_t\right]$ — expected return under the *learned* model.
- Planning operator $\phi \leftarrow \phi + \eta \nabla_\phi J(\phi)$ by backprop through the unrolled $M_\theta$ (reparameterized or score-function gradients).
- Curiosity unit: intrinsic reward $r_t^{\text{int}} = \| s_{t+1} - \mathbb{E}_{M_\theta}[s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t] \|^2$, added to extrinsic reward in $J$, with $M_\theta$ trained to drive this error to zero.

Closing comparison: Dyna's model is a tabular Dirac and planning is $K$ tabular Q-updates per real step; Schmidhuber's model is a differentiable network and planning is gradient descent through the unrolled $M_\theta$. Outer loop is the same; model class and planner differ.

## §3 walkthrough — Dyna-Q algorithm box

Five-step algorithm:

1. Observe $s$, select $a \in \arg\max_{a'} Q(s, a')$ with $\varepsilon$-greedy.
2. Execute $a$, observe $(r, s')$.
3. Direct RL update: $Q(s, a) \leftarrow Q(s, a) + \alpha[r + \gamma \max_{a''} Q(s', a'') - Q(s, a)]$.
4. Model update: $\widehat r(s, a) \leftarrow r$, $\widehat P(\cdot \mid s, a) \leftarrow \delta_{s'}$.
5. Planning: repeat $K$ times — sample previously-visited $(\tilde s, \tilde a)$, query $\widehat r, \widehat P$, apply same Q-update.

Step 3 is the Watkins-Dayan 1992 update; converges to $Q^\star$ under decreasing-step-size conditions; Bellman optimality operator is a $\gamma$-contraction in sup norm. Step 5 applies the same update with model-generated tuples. Full planning-and-learning loop has no closed-form convergence guarantee in the tabular case beyond consistency of $\widehat P, \widehat r$ under the visitation distribution. Function-approximation case studied via Lipschitz-on-model arguments (Asadi 2018, cross-ref to §10).

Two architectural commitments isolated at the end of §3.3:

1. **Model class is unspecified.** This is the slot that PlaNet/Dreamer (RSSM), MuZero (value-aware latent), MBPO (deep ensemble), and others fill.
2. **Inner loop applies the same Bellman update on simulated and real data.** Step 5 is not a distinct operator; it is Step 3 with a different tuple source. The ratio $K$ tunes the mixture.

## §3.4 — Dyna maze sim specifics

### Environment

- Grid: 6 rows × 9 columns. Start $S = (5, 3)$, goal $G = (0, 8)$. Reward +1 on goal arrival, 0 elsewhere.
- Wall row 2. Phase 1 wall cols $\{0, ..., 7\}$ (opening at col 8). Phase 2 wall cols $\{1, ..., 8\}$ (opening at col 0). Flip at $t = 1000$, not signaled.
- $\gamma = 0.95$, episode cap 200 steps, total budget 3000 env steps.

### Why-Q-learning-is-slow diagnosis paragraph

Pre-Setup paragraph quantifies the slow propagation:
- Random walk hitting time from $S$ to $G$ on the open part of the 54-cell grid is several hundred steps.
- Each goal-reach updates one $Q$-entry by $\alpha \cdot 1 = 0.1$ toward $\gamma^{d-1}$.
- Propagating signal back to $S$ requires chain of subsequent goal-reaches.
- $\varepsilon = 0.1$ diverts the agent off currently-best path one in ten steps; chance of stringing together long sequences in a few hundred env steps is small.
- This is the precise form of "slow per-step propagation" that Dyna-Q is designed to address.

### Five agents

1. **Q-learning ($K = 0$)** — direct-RL baseline.
2. **Dyna-Q ($K = 5$)** — five model-based replays per real step from tabular Dirac model.
3. **Dyna-Q ($K = 50$)** — fifty replays. Headline of Sutton 1990.
4. **Dyna-Q+ ($K = 50$, $\kappa = 10^{-4}$)** — Sutton-Barto Ch 8.3 curiosity bonus $\kappa \sqrt{\tau(s, a)}$ on planning targets. User's improvement (May 2026): also registers untried actions at every visited state with $t_\text{last} = 0$, so the bonus drives the agent toward untried actions (proper S&B implementation rather than the limited "only-revisit-stale" variant).
5. **Schmidhuber 1990 (NN)** — small torch MLP `WorldModel` (state+action → next-state logits + reward head, both jointly fit on cross-entropy + MSE) plus MLP controller fit by REINFORCE through imagined rollouts under $M$. Hidden dim 32, Adam lr 3e-3, replay buffer cap 10k, planning every 10 real steps with 16 imagined trajectories of horizon 10, moving-average baseline, entropy coefficient 0.01.

Verdict: tabular Dyna dominates this small maze, gradient-based architecture's promise visible only when the state space is large enough that tabular methods cannot follow.

## §5 walkthrough — Recurrent State-Space Model and Dreamer line

Two focused subsubsections (§5.1, §5.2) plus a one-paragraph history (§5.3) — the latter now compressed into a footnote at the end of §5.2.

### §5.1 RSSM

Latent state factored as deterministic recurrent $h_t$ and stochastic latent $s_t$:

$$h_t = f_\theta(h_{t-1}, s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}), \qquad s_t \sim p_\theta(s_t \mid h_t).$$

$p_\theta$ is Gaussian (PlaNet, V1) or product of categoricals (V2, V3) — design choice, not structural commitment. Posterior network $q_\theta(s_t \mid h_t, o_t)$ for variational training. Joint loss: reconstruction + reward log-likelihood + KL prior-posterior consistency, optionally with latent-overshooting consistency.

### §5.2 Actor-critic in imagination

Actor $\pi_\phi(a_t \mid h_t, s_t)$, critic $V_\psi(h_t, s_t)$ trained on imagined trajectories rolled forward through the latent dynamics, starting from real states drawn from the replay buffer. Imagined return objective:

$$J(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\phi, p_\theta}\!\left[ \sum_{\tau = t}^{t+H} \gamma^{\tau - t} \hat r_\tau + \gamma^{H+1} V_\psi(h_{t+H+1}, s_{t+H+1}) \right].$$

Value gradients via backprop through the unrolled latent. Critic fit by squared error against $\lambda$-return targets. Convergence: no closed form; nearest analogue is Asadi Lipschitz bound (cross-ref to §10).

### §5.3 (now in footnote)

PlaNet (HafnerPlaNet2019) introduced RSSM + cross-entropy planning. DreamerV1 (HafnerDreamer2020) replaced planning with actor-critic in imagination. DreamerV2 (HafnerDreamerV2_2021) categorical latents + KL balancing trick. DreamerV3 (HafnerDreamerV3_2025) single hyperparameter configuration across 150+ tasks via symlog reward normalization and free-bits KL, plus Minecraft diamond from scratch.

## §6 walkthrough — Value-aware model learning

### §6.1 VAML loss

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAML}}(\widehat P) = \sup_{V \in \mathcal{V}} \| (\widehat P V)(\cdot) - (P^\star V)(\cdot) \|_\mu.$$

Penalizes worst-case discrepancy in expected next-state value across value function class $\mathcal{V}$. A model that distorts pixels but preserves value is not penalized; a model that distorts value expectation is. IterVAML (Farahmand 2018) embeds this in approximate value iteration. Wasserstein-VAML (Asadi 2018) reads it through optimal transport under a Lipschitz assumption on the value class.

### §6.2 Value equivalence and MuZero

Two transition kernels $\widehat P, P^\star$ are **value-equivalent** w.r.t. policy set $\Pi$ and value set $\mathcal{V}$ when their Bellman operators agree on $\Pi \times \mathcal{V}$. Equivalence class shrinks monotonically as $\Pi, \mathcal{V}$ grow, collapsing to $\{P^\star\}$ in the limit. Proper value equivalence (GrimmPVE2021) restricts the value class to fixed points of the chosen Bellman operators.

VTR regret (AyoubVTR2020) trains the model against value targets and matches dimension-dependent rates of optimism-based RL.

MuZero (Schrittwieser2020MuZero): latent recurrence trained against three losses — reward prediction, value prediction, policy prediction (matched to MCTS output rooted at the same latent). No reconstruction loss; latent dynamics never asked to decode observations. Stochastic MuZero (Antonoglou 2022) extends with afterstate node.

Footnotes:
- Voelcker 2025 calibration: standard MuZero losses are uncalibrated surrogates; minima don't coincide with true value-function minima under unrestricted model class. Open problem on policy drift.
- Donti-Amos-Kolter 2017 decision-focused learning parallel: same "align loss with downstream decision" move, with convex program rather than Bellman backup as inner solver.

## §7 walkthrough — MBPO and the modern Dyna

Single focused subsubsection (after the 80/20 cut).

Branched short rollouts: at each gradient step, sample a real state $s$ from the replay buffer, roll the learned dynamics ensemble forward for $k$ steps from $s$ under the current policy, train the actor-critic on the resulting synthetic transitions alongside real ones. Actor-critic doesn't distinguish synthetic from real.

Janner Theorem 4.2 bound:

$$\eta[\pi] \geq \eta^{\text{branch}}[\pi] - 2 r_{\max}\Big[\frac{\gamma^{k+1}\epsilon_\pi}{(1-\gamma)^2} + \frac{\gamma^k + 2}{1-\gamma}\epsilon_\pi + \frac{k}{1-\gamma}(\epsilon_m + 2\epsilon_\pi)\Big].$$

First two terms penalize policy shift (shrink in $k$); third penalizes model error (grows linearly in $k$). Theorem 4.3: $\exists k^\star > 0$ optimal when model error is small enough. Practical takeaway: $k = 1$ competitive, $k \in [5, 15]$ typically best, $k > 25$ too inaccurate.

Ensemble (typically 5-7 networks predicting Gaussian over next-state delta) plays two roles: calibrated aggregate predictor, and disagreement signal for epistemic uncertainty.

Footnotes:
- ME-TRPO (Kurutach 2018): on-policy TRPO; bounds $\epsilon_\pi$ explicitly but keeps long-horizon model dependence.
- PETS (Chua 2018): MPC with no policy learning; conflates task and planning horizon, limits scaling.
- Empirical: MuJoCo, MBPO matches SAC at ~10× fewer env samples. Humanoid: MBPO converges, PETS fails. ME-TRPO ~2 orders of magnitude better than TRPO/PPO/DDPG.

## §8 walkthrough — TD-MPC2

Five jointly trained components:
- Encoder $h$: observation $s_t \to z_t = h(s_t)$.
- Latent dynamics $d$: $\hat z_{t+1} = d(z_t, a_t)$.
- Reward model $R$: $\hat r_t = R(z_t, a_t)$.
- Terminal Q-function $Q$: $\hat q_t = Q(z_t, a_t)$.
- Policy prior $p$: $\hat a_t = p(z_t)$.

Joint loss: latent consistency $\| d(z_t, a_t) - \text{sg}(h(s_{t+1})) \|^2$ (stop-gradient on target), cross-entropy on reward, cross-entropy on TD targets, behavioral cloning of $p$ on planner-selected actions. **No reconstruction loss.**

MPPI planner with value bootstrap:

$$\mu^\star, \sigma^\star = \arg\max_{(\mu, \sigma)} \mathbb{E}_{(a_t, ..., a_{t+H}) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)}\Big[\gamma^H Q(z_{t+H}, a_{t+H}) + \sum_{h=t}^{H-1} \gamma^{h-t} R(z_h, a_h)\Big].$$

Terminal $Q$ extends planner's effective horizon past finite window $H$; sidesteps PETS-style long-rollout compounding error.

Empirical: 104 tasks across DMC/Meta-World/ManiSkill2/MyoSuite under single hyperparameter config. Beats SAC, original TD-MPC, DreamerV3. Scaling: 1M → 317M parameters, normalized score 16.0 → 70.6, log-linear, no saturation.

## §10 walkthrough — synthesis

Four subsubsections:

### §10.1 Where MBRL wins

Sample efficiency. Model-based methods outperform model-free baselines when environment interaction is scarce or expensive. Pixel-based continuous control (PlaNet, Dreamer). Discrete-state planning (MuZero). Continuous control with sparse reward (MBPO, TD-MPC2). Model serves as data amplifier (Dyna planning step) and representation amplifier (RSSM/TD-MPC2 latents transfer across tasks). When transferable representations or finite-data regimes are the binding constraint, model-based methods win.

### §10.2 Where MBRL fails

Two failure modes:

1. **Misspecification + objective mismatch.** Lambert 2020: likelihood-reward correlations as low as 0.07 on Half-Cheetah; adversarial perturbations preserve likelihood while halving return. Voelcker 2025: value-equivalent representations miscalibrated under distribution shift. Value-aware line is partial answer; off-distribution calibration remains open.
2. **Compounding error + data-coverage gaps.** Asadi 2018 Lipschitz bound: value error grows geometrically at rate $K_F$ (learned-dynamics Lipschitz constant); finite when $\gamma K_F < 1$, diverges otherwise. Talvitie 2017: retraining a model on its own predictions can destabilize it (hallucinated-rollout fix). Pathak 2017 + Sekar 2020: prediction-error-driven curiosity and ensemble-disagreement-driven planning push the data distribution beyond greedy exploitation.

### §10.3 When the model is not needed

Madeka 2022: deep inventory management, supervised reduction matches or beats learned-world-model pipeline at lower engineering cost. The boundary is closed-loop feedback: when the agent's action doesn't feed back into the data-generating process, supervised reduction suffices; when it does, the learned model becomes load-bearing.

### §10.4 Open questions

The dual simulation maps where MBRL sits on the sample-efficiency frontier. In the cobweb setting, the Marcet-Sargent divergence-in-unstable-region story doesn't arise (single-agent, prices not expectations). Whether the divergence story would reappear in an expectational cobweb is open. The analogy to E-stability is suggestive rather than formal; operators, fixed points, and misspecification objects differ. An economist-style stability theory for MBRL in economic environments remains to be built.

Closing monograph bridge: this chapter is where the simulator dependence of reinforcement learning becomes explicit. The agent's policy is only as good as the model it plans against, and the question of when a learned model is accurate enough for an economic policy counterfactual is the open one the monograph carries into its conclusion.

## Sim code reuse patterns

All three sim scripts share a common skeleton (`Paradigm` base class, `compute_or_load` per paradigm via `sims.sim_cache`, factory function for paradigm instantiation, `rollout` loop, `generate_outputs` with figure + table + stdout). The patterns to copy when extending:

- `Paradigm` base class with `reset(params, seed)`, `act(state, t)`, `observe(state, action, reward, next_state)`, and optional `get_params()` / `greedy_action(*ref_state)` for deep-analysis dimensions.
- Per-paradigm config dicts inherit from `SHARED_CONFIG` (env params, gamma, T, seeds) and add their own hyperparameters. Each config dict is hashed by `compute_or_load` to invalidate cache when params change.
- `PARADIGM_REGISTRY = {name: (compute_paradigm, config)}` and `PARADIGM_ORDER = [...]` (rank-ordered). Figure legend and table writer iterate over `PARADIGM_ORDER`.
- Output naming: `<script_name>.png` (main figure), `<script_name>_results.tex` (main table), `<script_name>_stdout.txt` (run log), `<script_name>_audit.md` (pre-code 7-point audit).
- Cache files at `<output_dir>/cache/<script_name>__<paradigm_name>.pkl` (with spaces replaced by underscores).

## Compile troubleshooting

- **`§??` in standalone PDF.** Forward references to `section:rl_algorithms` (ch02 algorithms chapter) only resolve in the full-monograph compile. Decision is to leave as-is; the full monograph PDF renders correctly.
- **Stale figure or table after sim change.** Run `python3 sims/<script>.py --plots-only` to regenerate from cache without re-running the agents.
- **Cache invalidation.** Changing a paradigm's hyperparameter triggers re-run of only that paradigm. Changing `SHARED_CONFIG` (env params, seed count, T) cascades to re-run everything. To force re-run without changing config: `python3 sims/<script>.py --algo "Paradigm Name"`.
- **`--plots-only` writes table file with stale order.** The plots-only flag re-runs `generate_outputs` from cache, so it picks up the latest `PARADIGM_ORDER`. If the table comes out in the wrong order, check that `PARADIGM_ORDER` was edited in the script before running.
- **Compile order:** pdflatex once → bibtex → pdflatex twice. The first pdflatex generates `.aux`, bibtex resolves citations, second pdflatex picks up cited keys, third pdflatex resolves cross-references and finalizes the TOC.
- **Cobweb sim slow.** The 7-paradigm version with MBPO + REINFORCE adds substantial compute. Use `--algo` to re-run only changed paradigms; --plots-only is fast (just reads cache).
- **Fishery sim slow.** DP refits inside RLS and MBPO dominate cost. Inner DP grid is intentionally coarser (25 × 15) than the oracle's (50 × 25). REFIT_EVERY is 25 observations to keep the total DP-solve count manageable. Don't reduce REFIT_EVERY without checking total compute.

## Style guide quick reference

- No `\textbf{}` anywhere in body prose (math carries weight; `\emph{}` sparingly).
- No em-dashes. Use commas, semicolons, or period splits.
- No colons in prose (math equations can have `:` of course).
- No bullet points in body LaTeX outside numbered lists where structurally necessary.
- No `\paragraph*{}` in ordinary prose. Exception: `\paragraph*{Setup.}`, `\paragraph*{Models.}`, `\paragraph*{Results.}`, `\paragraph*{Why X fails.}` in sim subsubsections.
- Hedge econ↔ML parallels with "in the same family as" / "structurally parallel" / "echoes the".
- Tables and figure legends in rank order by performance.
- Each sim ends with one explicit verdict sentence at the close of Results.
- After each major RL method section, one short economics-threading sentence ("Read in economics terms, ...").
