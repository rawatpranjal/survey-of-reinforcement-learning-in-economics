# Economist Readability Notes: World Models Chapter

## Main Diagnosis

The chapter is strong on substance, but it asks economists to absorb too many RL architecture terms before the core idea is stable.

The core idea should be simple:

A world model is an estimated transition-and-reward forecaster. The agent uses it to simulate possible futures and improve its policy. The central question is when those simulated futures are accurate enough for the decision problem.

Everything else should be organized around that idea.

## Core Terms To Define Upfront

Use only this small vocabulary as required chapter vocabulary:

- World model: an estimated model of next states and rewards.
- Planning update: a policy or value update performed using the learned model.
- Simulated rollout: a sequence generated by the learned model rather than by the real environment.
- Latent state: an internal compressed state used by a neural model; it is not an observed economic state.
- Model loss: the criterion used to train the world model.
- Value-aware loss: a model loss that cares about preserving values, not predicting every detail.
- Short rollout: a deliberately short simulation from a real visited state.
- Bootstrap: using a value estimate to stand in for rewards beyond the simulated horizon.
- Model error: the gap between the learned model and the true environment, judged by its effect on planning.

## Where Economists Will Trip

1. They may read "world model" as a structural model with identification content.
   Clarify that most RL world models are predictive planning objects, not identified economic structures.

2. They may treat simulated rollouts as valid counterfactuals.
   Clarify that a rollout is only as credible as the learned model in the region being used.

3. They may confuse likelihood accuracy with decision accuracy.
   This is the chapter's central conceptual distinction and should appear before VAML, MuZero, and TD-MPC2.

4. They may not understand that "latent" means internal representation.
   Say explicitly that latent states are learned summaries used for control, not directly interpretable economic states.

5. They may see Dreamer, MuZero, MBPO, and TD-MPC2 as acronym clutter.
   Present them as answers to three questions:
   - Can the agent plan inside a learned latent model?
   - Should the model be trained for prediction or for value accuracy?
   - How far can the agent trust learned dynamics?

6. They may overread the cobweb simulation as an E-stability result.
   Keep the caveat that the simulation is a single-agent control comparison, not a formal adaptive-learning stability theorem.

## Simplification Strategy

Define the small vocabulary before the technical sections.

In the Ha-Schmidhuber section, make the main text say:
The agent compresses pixels, forecasts the next compressed state, and trains a controller on simulated rollouts.

Move VAE, ELBO, mixture-density RNN, LSTM, and evolution strategies into footnotes or short parentheticals.

In the RSSM section, explain before the equation:
The model carries one variable for remembered history and one variable for uncertainty about the current state.

In the value-aware section, lead with:
A likelihood-trained model asks whether it predicts observations well. A value-aware model asks whether it preserves the next-state values needed for control.

In the MBPO section, lead with:
MBPO is the "do not trust the model too far" method. It starts simulations from real states and keeps them short.

In the TD-MPC2 section, lead with:
TD-MPC2 combines a latent model, a value-aware training target, short-horizon planning, and a terminal value bootstrap.

## Footnotes To Add

Add footnotes for critical context only:

- KL divergence: a discrepancy penalty between two distributions.
- ELBO: the standard VAE training objective combining reconstruction and a latent penalty.
- Reparameterization: the trick that lets gradients pass through stochastic latent draws.
- Bootstrap: a value estimate used beyond the explicit simulated horizon.
- MPPI: a sampling-based model-predictive-control method.
- Value equivalence: equivalence relative to chosen policy and value classes, not global model correctness.
- Simulated rollouts: useful for policy search but not automatically valid structural counterfactuals.

## Style Rules

Do not add analogies.

Do not introduce new acronyms unless they are paper names.

Prefer "estimated forecaster," "simulated rollout," "internal state," and "decision-relevant loss" over architecture jargon where possible.

Keep equations, but give the reader the economic role of each equation before showing it.
