## Notes

1. **`refs_extended.bib` is not loaded at compile time.** `docs/main.tex` specifies `\bibliography{refs}`, so entries in `refs_extended.bib` are never used in the compiled PDF even when cited. If any chapter intends to cite an entry that exists only in `refs_extended.bib`, that citation will fail to resolve. Currently no such case exists (Section 1 is empty), but the 189 duplicates and many orphans in `refs_extended.bib` indicate it was used as a staging area and should either be merged into `refs.bib` selectively or kept as a reference-only file.

2. **Duplicate resolution priority.** For the 189 duplicated keys, BibTeX will silently use whichever definition it encounters first. Since `\bibliography{refs}` is the only loaded file, the `refs.bib` copy is authoritative and the `refs_extended.bib` copies are never seen. No compile-time conflict exists at present.

3. **Orphan entries in `refs.bib`.** The 197 orphaned entries in `refs.bib` (those not in `refs_extended.bib` only) are defined in the loaded bib file but uncited. They do not cause errors but inflate the file. They may represent preparatory entries for chapters still being written or entries from cut chapters.

4. **In-chapter key variants.** Several keys appear in multiple near-identical variants (e.g., `daskalakis2009complexity` and `daskalakis2009complexity_dup1`; `brown2019_deepcfr` and `brown2019deepcfr` and `brown2019deepcfr_icml`; `cao2021` and `cao2021_identifiability` and `cao2021irl`). These are not duplicates in the strict sense (different keys) but likely refer to the same or related papers and may warrant consolidation.
